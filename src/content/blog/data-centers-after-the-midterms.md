**Dispatch — Not an Investigation**

The November 3 elections brought data-center development before voters in several communities. According to Ballotpedia's November 6 analysis, outcomes favored restrictions or opposed development in 40 of 41 local ballot measures across ten states.

Ballotpedia reports that voters approved 27 measures restricting or opposing data-center development, including five outright bans, and rejected 13 measures favoring development. Harwood, North Dakota, was the exception.

Those numbers require context. Ohio accounted for 21 of the 41 measures, according to Ballotpedia's state-by-state breakdown. Ballotpedia classifies nine of the 40 outcomes as nonbinding. It also described the results as unofficial. I have not checked certification status.

The figures describe measures that reached local ballots. They do not establish national public opinion or explain why individual voters made their choices.

In Texas, Gina Hinojosa, the governor-elect following a race call by the Associated Press, made a separate commitment during her campaign. According to multiple news reports quoting an October 22 video, she said she would impose a moratorium on data-center approvals until the Legislature passes protections for Texans. She has not yet taken office. Her statement describes an intended action, not an order already issued.

OpenAI has published a November 2026 document titled *AI Infrastructure That Works for America: Our Compute Campus Policy Blueprint*, reported in the press as a National Community Compact. The document identifies ratepayer protection among its commitments for campuses it owns and operates. It also states that OpenAI will publish site-specific information on its website, including noise measurements, energy use, and water use.

The company says it will not require public officials to sign nondisclosure agreements moving forward, except, as OpenAI describes it, in very narrow cases involving legitimate security information, trade secrets, or proprietary or competitively sensitive information.

Separately, the document recommends that policymakers require standardized reporting of electricity and water use, noise studies, and other measures. These proposals go beyond OpenAI's own publication commitments. The distinction matters: the document contains both commitments made by the company and recommendations for government action. This dispatch does not assess whether those commitments have been implemented or the proposals adopted.

These developments concern different institutions, jurisdictions, and forms of decision-making. This dispatch does not determine their legal force or effectiveness, nor does it suggest that the election caused OpenAI's publication.

We plan to examine the scope of these measures and commitments, the authorities responsible for implementing them, and the evidence available to verify their consequences. That investigation may produce mixed or inconclusive findings.

For today, the distinction is simple: **reporting what was decided or announced is not the same as establishing what will happen.**

### Editorial Note: Why We Published a Dispatch

This morning, we faced a difficult editorial decision.

We had been considering an essay about how data-center rules actually form, alongside an investigation into whether announced data-center protections become enforceable outcomes. Both appeared relevant to today's publication schedule.

But our review revealed unresolved questions. Important government records remained unread, and we had not established the consequences of several decisions and commitments. We also identified a significant distinction between a company's own commitments and its proposals for government action.

We considered revising the existing essay, postponing publication, and publishing a narrower account.

We chose the narrower account.

This dispatch reports what our sources presently support. It does not substitute for the unfinished investigations.

We believe readers deserve to know not only what we publish, but why we sometimes decide not to publish something else.
